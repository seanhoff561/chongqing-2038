When it comes to story design: Design the game around the core principles of Outer Wilds: curiosity-driven exploration, environmental storytelling, and knowledge-based progression. Begin with mysteries the player can observe and genuinely want to investigate, rather than exposition, cutscenes, or explicit objectives. Every meaningful discovery should answer a question, raise another, challenge an earlier assumption, or reveal something the player can act on. Information is the primary reward: learning about a hidden entrance, recognizing a landmark, or understanding how a system works should change what the player can accomplish. Knowledge should unlock possibilities naturally, not through invisible quest flags. A player who figures something out independently should be able to act on it without first finding the intended clue. Build an interconnected web of discoveries rather than a linear sequence of quests. Each location should have a distinct history, purpose, and physical layout that naturally explains what evidence exists there and why. Tell stories through architecture, objects, documents, environmental changes, and the traces people leave behind. Every clue should feel like something that genuinely belongs in the world, created by someone for a believable reason rather than placed to instruct the player. Connect mysteries across locations so discoveries lead players to investigate elsewhere, reinterpret familiar places, and gradually reconstruct events in whatever order they choose. Important information should have multiple discoverable paths, allowing players to miss clues, explore out of order, and still make progress. Reward curiosity with meaningful revelations, not endless breadcrumbs. Every significant location should provide a satisfying discovery, whether it is an answer, a useful navigational insight, or a memorable human detail. Design spaces and their stories together, using recognizable landmarks, alternative entrances, obstacles, sightlines, and environmental clues to encourage observation, deduction, and experimentation. Preserve player agency by making exploration optional, discoveries persistent, and interpretations their own. The ideal experience is one where the player thinks: "I noticed something, formed a theory, went somewhere to test it, and discovered something that changed my understanding of the world."

### Rendering and performance
